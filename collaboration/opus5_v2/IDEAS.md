## A. The main new line: disjointness parity

### A.1 The reduction (PROVED-HERE, `PROOF.md` Lemma 0.2)

A tight \((k+1)\)-colouring of \(J(2k,k)\) is exactly \(p=k+1\) **pairwise
block-disjoint** \(S(k-1,k,2k)\) systems.  Therefore, for every even \(k\ge4\):

> **If no three \(S(k-1,k,2k)\) systems are pairwise block-disjoint, then
> Problem #835 has a negative answer at that \(k\).**

Because \(p\ge5\), we do not need "no large set"; we only need "no triangle in
the disjointness graph".  This is a *much* stronger statement than required,
which is what makes it a plausible target: it may be provable by a local
argument, whereas the large-set statement is global.

**Prior art (checked after writing §A.1):** this reduction is already recorded
in this repository as the *two-disjoint conjecture* from an earlier session,
which also verified computationally that the Fano disjointness graph is
8-regular and triangle-free and that the maximum number of pairwise disjoint
\(S(4,5,11)\) is 2, and which pushed the related parity conjectures P and E a
long way.  §A.1 is therefore **not new**.  What is new below is the proposed
mechanism (§A.3), the hand evidence for it (§A.4), the refutation of the
obvious sharpening (§A.4b) and the proved fragment (§A.4c).

### A.2 It is true in every settled case (LITERATURE)

| \(k\) | critical system | max pairwise disjoint | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | \(S(1,2,4)\) | 3 \(=k+1\) | trivial, and #835 *is* solvable at \(k=2\) |
| 4 | \(S(3,4,8)\) | 2 | repo `verify_k4.py` (30 labelled systems) |
| 4 | \(S(2,3,7)\) (derived form) | 2 | classical |
| 6 | \(S(5,6,12)\) | 2 | Kramer–Mesner, cited in the repo note |

The jump from \(3\) at \(k=2\) to \(2\) at \(k=4,6\) is the whole phenomenon.
Any correct uniform proof has to explain why \(k=2\) is different.

### A.3 The candidate mechanism: a sign-equivariant \(\pm1\) invariant

**CONJECTURE (Disjointness–Parity).**  For even \(k\ge4\) there is a map
\(\epsilon\) from \(S(k-1,k,2k)\) systems to \(\{\pm1\}\) with
\[
 \epsilon(\pi\mathcal D)=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\,\epsilon(\mathcal D)
 \quad(\pi\in S_{2k}),
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_1\cap\mathcal D_2=\varnothing\ \Rightarrow\
 \epsilon(\mathcal D_1)\epsilon(\mathcal D_2)=-1 .
\]

Given such an \(\epsilon\), the disjointness graph is bipartite, so it has no
triangle, so A.1 finishes the problem at that \(k\).  The conjecture is
consistent with \(k=2\) failing: the stabiliser of a perfect matching of
\([4]\) in \(S_4\) is \(D_8\not\le A_4\), so no sign-equivariant \(\epsilon\)
exists there, and indeed three pairwise disjoint systems do exist.

**Necessary condition for \(\epsilon\) to exist at all:**
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal D)\le A_{2k}\) for every system \(\mathcal D\).
This is known in the settled cases (\(\mathrm{AGL}(3,2)\le A_8\) and
\(M_{12}\le A_{12}\), both because the groups are generated by even elements).
It is **open** for \(k=16,18\), and it is exactly the kind of statement the
repository's "every nonidentity involution of a hypothetical \(LS(4,5,21)\)
fixes exactly one point" result is evidence for: such an involution has cycle
type \(2^{10}1\), which is even.

### A.4 Hand-verified special case: the Fano/\(\mathbb F_8\) family
**(HAND-CHECKED-SPECIAL-CASE, complete and self-contained)**

Take the Fano plane \(F\) on the point set \(\mathbb F_8^{\times}\) with lines
\(\{x,y,z\}\), \(x+y+z=0\).  For \(\gcd(m,7)=1\) let \(\pi_m(x)=x^m\), a
permutation of the 7 points.  Then, in characteristic \(2\):

* \(m=2,4\): Frobenius powers, \(\pi_m\) preserves every line.
* \(m=3\): \(x^3+y^3+(x+y)^3=xy(x+y)\ne0\).
* \(m=5\): \(x^5+y^5+(x+y)^5=xy(x+y)(x^2+xy+y^2)\ne0\), because
  \(t^2+t+1\) has no root in \(\mathbb F_8\) (its roots lie in \(\mathbb F_4\)).
* \(m=6\): \(x^{-1}+y^{-1}+(x+y)^{-1}=\dfrac{x^2+xy+y^2}{xy(x+y)}\ne0\).

So \(\pi_m F\) is **block-disjoint** from \(F\) exactly for \(m\in\{3,5,6\}\)
and equal to \(F\) for \(m\in\{1,2,4\}\).

Signs: writing points as \(g^{e}\), \(\pi_m\) acts on the exponents
\(\mathbb Z_7\) as multiplication by \(m\); it fixes \(e=0\) and splits the
other six exponents into \(6/d\) cycles of length \(d=\operatorname{ord}_7(m)\),
so \(\operatorname{sgn}\pi_m=(-1)^{6/d}\).  With
\(d(1,2,3,4,5,6)=(1,3,6,3,6,2)\):
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}\pi_m=+1 \iff m\in\{1,2,4\},\qquad
 \operatorname{sgn}\pi_m=-1 \iff m\in\{3,5,6\}.
\]

**Even \(\iff\) line-preserving; odd \(\iff\) block-disjoint**, exactly as the
conjecture predicts, on the nose, for this whole family.  This is genuine
evidence, not a proof: it only covers the \(6\) power maps out of \(5040\)
permutations.

### A.4b A tempting stronger form that is FALSE (hand-refuted here)

It is natural to guess the sharper statement

> \(|F\cap\pi F|\) is odd iff \(\pi\) is even.

That would give A.3 immediately, since a disjoint pair has \(|F\cap\pi F|=0\).
**It is false.**  Take the Fano plane
\(F=\{123,145,167,246,257,347,356\}\) on \([7]\) and the odd permutation
\(\pi=(6\,7)\).  Then \(\pi\) fixes the lines \(123,145,167\) and sends
\(246,257,347,356\) to \(247,256,346,357\), none of which is a line of \(F\).
So \(|F\cap\pi F|=3\), odd, with \(\pi\) odd.

This refutes the parity-of-intersection mechanism but says nothing against
A.3 itself (the pair is not disjoint).  Recorded so the same guess is not
made again.

### A.4c A proved fragment of the necessary condition

`PROOF.md` Theorem 2.3: for even \(k\), no \(S(k-1,k,2k)\) has a transposition
as an automorphism.  Proof is two lines from \(N_{k-1}=0\).  This is the first
nontrivial step towards \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal D)\le A_{2k}\), which
A.3 requires.  Extending it past transpositions is open: the argument works
for any \(\pi\) forcing some block \(A\) with \(|A\cap\pi A|\in\{1,k-1\}\), and
that is exactly what genuine automorphisms such as \(\mathrm{AGL}(3,2)\) avoid.

### A.5 What would have to be checked next (blocked here: no compute)

`verify_disjointness_parity.py` in this directory does the decisive finite
test, for \(S(2,3,7)\), \(S(3,4,8)\) and \(S(4,5,11)\):

1. generate all labelled systems by orbit-BFS under adjacent transpositions,
   carrying a parity tag;
2. detect whether any system is reachable with both parities (that would mean
   \(\operatorname{Aut}\not\le A_n\) and would kill A.3 at once);
3. count block-disjoint pairs split by same-orbit / different-orbit;
4. compute the maximum clique of the disjointness graph.

**If step 3 finds a single same-orbit disjoint pair, the conjecture is false
as stated and this whole line dies.**  That is the honest, cheap falsification
test and it has not been run.

### A.6 The \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear form of the same target

\(\epsilon\) of the special form \(\epsilon(\mathcal D)=(-1)^{|W\cap\mathcal D|}\)
for a fixed \(W\subseteq\binom{[2k]}{k}\) would do.  Note:

* \(W\) only matters modulo the \(\mathbb F_2\)-row space of the inclusion
  matrices \(W_{i,k}(2k)\), \(i\le k-1\), because those functionals are
  *constant* on all Steiner systems (design property).  So \(W\) must be a
  genuinely non-design functional.
* The existence of such a \(W\) is strictly stronger than triangle-freeness:
  three pairwise disjoint systems give
  \(d_{12}+d_{13}+d_{23}=0\) with \(d_{ab}=\chi_a+\chi_b\), so
  "no odd subset of the difference set sums to \(0\)" and "no triangle" are
  the same condition at length \(3\).  Exhibiting an explicit \(W\) is
  therefore a proof strategy, not a weakening.

## B. The local-sign lever that is still open

`PROOF.md` §4 shows the *global* star sign \(E(c)\) is invariant under points
and colours, and that its "dual" evaluation is literally the same function, so
comparing up- and down-stars cannot produce a contradiction.  But the
following exact identity survives and is **not** closed.

**PROVED-HERE (identity).**  For a \((k-2)\)-set \(T\), let \(L_T\) be the
symmetric Latin square of order \(p+1\) on \(P=[2k]\setminus T\) given by
\(L_T(x,y)=c(T\cup\{x,y\})\) off the diagonal and \(\infty\) on it — i.e. the
1-factorization of \(K_{p+1}\) induced at \(T\).  Let
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}(L_T)=\prod_{x\in P}\operatorname{sgn}(\text{row }x)\).
Then
\[
 \prod_{x\in P}\epsilon(T\cup\{x\})=(-1)^{\binom{p+1}{2}}\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}(L_T),
 \qquad
 E(c)=(-1)^{\binom{2k}{k-2}\binom{p+1}{2}}\prod_{T\in\binom{[2k]}{k-2}}
      \epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}(L_T).
\]
(The second follows from the first because each \((k-1)\)-set \(B\) arises as
\(T\cup\{x\}\) for exactly \(k-1\) choices of \(x\), and \(k-1\) is odd.)

**PROVED-HERE.**  The *symbol* parity of \(L_T\) is forced:
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{symbol}}(L_T)=(-1)^{(p+1)/2}\), because the position map of
symbol \(c\in\mathbb Z_p\) is the fixed-point-free involution \(F_c\) on
\(p+1\) points and the position map of \(\infty\) is the identity.

**PROVED-HERE.**  \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}\) is invariant under relabelling
points and colours, and under switching the two colours on any connected
component of \(F_a\cup F_b\) (a cycle of length \(2\ell\) flips exactly
\(2\ell\) row signs).  So \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}\) is a genuine isomorphism
invariant of 1-factorizations that is blind to all cycle switches.

**OPEN / UNVERIFIED, and cheap to settle.**  Is
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}\) constant over all 1-factorizations of \(K_{n}\) for
even \(n\)?  It is trivially constant for \(n=4,6\) (one isomorphism class).
The first real test is \(n=8\) (six isomorphism classes).
`verify_onefactorization_row_parity.py` settles it.

* If \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}\) is **constant**, then \(E(c)\) is a fixed
  number depending only on \(k\), and §4's route is fully closed — a clean
  negative but a real one.
* If \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}\) is **not constant**, then
  \(T\mapsto\epsilon_{\mathrm{row}}(L_T)\) is a nontrivial \(\pm1\) field on
  \(\binom{[2k]}{k-2}\) attached to any tight colouring, and its consistency
  across overlapping \(T\)'s becomes a new finite obstruction to attack.  This
  is the single cheapest unexplored lever I found.

## C. Attacks I tried and can prove will not work

* **Chevalley–Warning on the power-sum system.**  The exact system is
  \(\sum_{A\supseteq B}c(A)^r=0\) for \(1\le r\le p-2\) and
  \(\sum_{A\supseteq B}c(A)^{p-1}=-1\), over all \((k-1)\)-sets \(B\).
  Number of variables \(N=\binom{2k}{k}\); the total degree is
  \(\binom{2k}{k-1}\cdot\frac{p(p-1)}{2}=N\frac{(p-1)^2}{2}\gg N\).  The
  hypothesis fails by a factor \(\Theta(k^2)\); no rescue by dropping
  equations, since dropping any \(r\) destroys the equivalence.
* **Mod-\(p\) rank of the inclusion matrix.**  By Wilson's \(p\)-rank theorem
  (LITERATURE), \(\operatorname{rank}_p W_{k-1,k}(2k)\) is determined by which
  \(i\le k-1\) satisfy \(p\nmid\binom{k-i}{k-1-i}=k-i\).  For \(p=k+1\) and
  \(0\le i\le k-1\) we have \(k-i\in\{1,\dots,k\}\), never divisible by \(p\),
  so the rank is **full**, \(\binom{2k}{k-1}\).  Characteristic \(p\) therefore
  gives exactly the same kernel dimension \(C_k\) as characteristic \(0\): no
  extra structure.  (The same computation shows the \(2\)-rank *is* deficient,
  which is why \(2\)-adic attacks look attractive; but Wilson's diagonal-form
  theorem makes \(Mx=\mathbf 1\) integrally solvable precisely under the
  divisibility conditions, so the \(2\)-rank alone cannot obstruct.)
* **Hoffman/ratio bound.**  \(J(2k,k)\) has eigenvalues \((k-i)^2-i\), least
  \(-k\) at \(i=k\); the ratio bound is \(\alpha\le\binom{2k}{k}\frac{k}{k^2+k}
  =\binom{2k}{k}/(k+1)\), attained exactly by a Steiner system.  So the bound
  is tight and gives \(\chi\ge k+1\) but never \(\ge k+2\).  Equality forces
  \(\chi_{\mathcal D}-\frac{1}{p}\mathbf 1\) into the top eigenspace
  \(S^{(k,k)}\) — the known Specht-module picture, no new content.
* **All single-block shell counts.**  `PROOF.md` Theorem 3.2 proves every one
  of them returns exactly \(r\le k+1\).  This is a *proof of the failure
  boundary*, so no amount of cleverness with one fixed block will help.
* **All additive colourings.**  `PROOF.md` Theorem 5.1, uniform in \(k\) and
  in the target group.

## D. On a positive construction at \(k=18\) (and later primes)

I did not attempt a numerical search (no compute).  What the theorems above
say about any such attempt:

* Any construction must be non-additive in the point labels
  (Theorem 5.1), and its colour classes must be **complement-closed**
  (Theorem 2.1) — this is now known for every single \(S(k-1,k,2k)\), not just
  for classes of a colouring, so a construction may as well be built on
  \(2k-1\) points as an \(S(k-2,k-1,2k-1)\) (Corollary 2.2).
* Its block-intersection distribution is completely forced:
  \(N_j=\binom kj\bigl(\binom kj+(-1)^{j}k\bigr)/(k+1)\) for even \(k\).  In
  particular no two blocks meet in exactly \(1\) or exactly \(k-1\) points.
  That is a strong, cheap filter for any candidate family.
* **Honest assessment (UNVERIFIED, but this is the literature position
  recorded in the repo note):** at \(k=18\) a single \(S(17,18,36)\) is not
  known to exist, and no Steiner system \(S(t,t+1,2t+2)\) is known for
  \(t\ge7\).  A construction at \(k=18\) would therefore first have to solve a
  well-known open existence problem, and then solve it \(19\) times
  disjointly.  I do not think this is the right place to spend effort, and I
  did not spend it there.
