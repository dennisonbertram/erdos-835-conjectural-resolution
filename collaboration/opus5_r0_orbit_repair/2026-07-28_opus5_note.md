## The cut invariant

Let \(G=K_{13}-D\).  For \(U\subseteq V\), put \(W=V\setminus U\).
For a remaining complement row \(R\), let \(S=V\setminus R\) be its support
and define

\[
\phi_U(R)=|S\cap U|-\frac{|S|}{2}.
\]

Suppose a perfect matching \(M\) on \(S\) has \(k\) edges inside \(U\),
\(m\) edges inside \(W\), and \(c\) crossing edges.  Counting endpoints on
the two sides gives

\[
|S\cap U|=2k+c,\qquad |S\cap W|=2m+c,
\]

and hence the exact per-row identity

\[
\boxed{\phi_U(R)=k-m.} \tag{1}
\]

For pairwise edge-disjoint matchings realizing a collection \(\mathcal R\)
of rows, (1) implies

\[
\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}\max(0,\phi_U(R))\le e(G[U]),\qquad
\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}\max(0,-\phi_U(R))\le e(G[W]). \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, if \(\phi_U(R)>0\), then the matching uses at least
\(\phi_U(R)\) edges of \(G[U]\), and these internal edges are distinct
across the selected rows.  The second inequality is symmetric.

For \(|U|=6\), the values simplify to

\[
\phi_U(R)=
\begin{cases}
1-|R\cap U|,& |R|=3,\\
2-|R\cap U|,& |R|=5.
\end{cases} \tag{3}
\]

## The all-row identity

Across all eleven remaining rows, the number of their supports containing a
vertex \(v\) is

\[
11-\rho(v)=12-d_D(v)=d_G(v).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_R\phi_U(R)
&=\sum_{v\in U}d_G(v)-\sum_R\frac{|S_R|}{2}\\
&=2e(G[U])+e_G(U,W)-51\\
&=e(G[U])-e(G[W]).
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]

The last equality uses \(|E(G)|=51\).  It follows that every
support-preserving repair, which leaves the row inventory fixed, preserves
the difference \(e(G[U])-e(G[W])\).  Also

\[
e(G[U])\le
\min\left\{\binom{|U|}{2},
\frac{51+\sum_R\phi_U(R)}2\right\}, \tag{5}
\]

with the symmetric bound for \(W\).  This bound is formally correct but
uninformative: substituting (4) into its second entry gives
\[
e(G[U])+\frac{e_G(U,W)}2.
\]
Thus (5) is only the handshake inequality.  The genuine selected-row
content is entirely in (2).

## What the invariant explains

In the committed all-35 obstruction there is a partition
\(V=U_6\mathbin{\dot\cup}W_7\) with

\[
D(U,W)=\varnothing,\qquad D[U]=K_6-\{e,f\},
\]

where \(e,f\) are disjoint.  Thus \(G[U]=\{e,f\}\).  Every remaining
size-ten row has its triple complement inside \(W\), so (3) gives
\(\phi_U=1\).  Three such rows require three distinct internal
\(U\)-edges by (2), but only two exist.  This reproves all 35 failures.

The support-preserving switch in the committed repair changes one old
\(U\)-edge and one old \(W\)-edge into two cross edges.  Consequently both
\(e(G[U])\) and \(e(G[W])\) increase by one while their difference stays
fixed.  The repaired graph has the exact three internal \(U\)-edges needed
by the three selected size-ten rows.

The exact row equations force all four remaining five-set complements to
lie in \(U\).  More precisely, their multiset is \(U\) minus each of the
four endpoints of \(e,f\), once each.  This forces the five-set portion of
the extremal inventory, not the entire labelled prefix or all seven triple
rows.

## The orbit-4 correction

The independently found orbit-4 obstruction has only twelve selected edges
on its densest six-set, so \(e(G[U])=3\).  The three target triple rows have
\(\phi_U=1\) each.  The cut inequality is tight rather than violated:

\[
1+1+1=e(G[U]).
\]

Nevertheless the three families have no mutually edge-disjoint triple.
This initially looked like evidence that (2) was insufficient.  A complete
audit over all \(2^{13}\) cuts corrected that conclusion.  For the different
six-set
\[
U=\{0,1,2,3,4,6\},
\]
the three values are \((-2,-2,-2)\), while
\[
e(G[V\setminus U])=5<6.
\]
So the full cut family (2) does detect orbit 4.

The same audit regenerated the targeted bad-prefix certificates in support
orbits \(2,\ldots,10\).  Every one violates (2), always on a six- or
seven-set; the numbers of violating oriented cuts are respectively
\[
2,2,2,2,4,2,2,4,2.
\]
This is strong exact evidence for cut sufficiency in the target profile, but
not a proof: one bad prefix per support orbit does not exhaust all bad
prefixes in that orbit.

The proposed crossing-Hall deficiency inequality was also checked.  It
detects none of these nine certificates and is therefore weaker than the
full cut family on the known obstruction census.

## Relation to the exact CEGIS proof route

The problem has quantifier order

\[
\forall\text{ valid input prefix}\quad
\exists\text{ same-support repair and continuation}.
\]

The companion CEGIS does not assert the negation of an unexpanded
existential witness.  Instead, every recorded witness defines a concrete
set of base assignments on which that same repair and continuation work,
and the added clause removes exactly that set.  If the exact base CNF
together with all such semantic clauses is UNSAT, their union covers every
valid base assignment.  This is an ordinary propositional proof of the
universal statement.  A theorem still requires frozen deterministic CNF
generation, an independently auditable witness log, and a replayed
CaDiCaL/DRAT-trim certificate.
