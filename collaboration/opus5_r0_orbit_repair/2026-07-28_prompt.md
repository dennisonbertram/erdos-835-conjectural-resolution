# Claude Opus 5 prompt: coordinated-nine \(r=0\)

We are working on a rigorous negative attack on Erdős--Rosenfeld Problem
#835.  The immediate bottleneck is the following finite graph theorem.
Please do the hard mathematical reasoning.  Seek a complete proof, not merely
an experiment, but use a finite SAT/DRAT reduction if that is the honest
remaining route.

Let \(V\) be a 13-point set.  Six pairwise edge-disjoint matchings
\(C_0,\ldots,C_5\) have sizes

\[
4,4,4,5,5,5.
\]

Let \(D=\bigcup_i C_i\).  There are eleven remaining complement rows:
seven triples \(T_0,\ldots,T_6\) and four five-sets
\(F_0,\ldots,F_3\).  Repetitions among equal-sized rows are allowed.  They
satisfy the exact class-B column equations

\[
\rho(v):=\#\{R\in\{T_i,F_j\}:v\in R\}=d_D(v)-1
\qquad(v\in V).
\]

Equivalently, the six prefix rows and eleven remaining rows form the
class-B complement inventory.  The support belonging to a row \(R\) is
\(V\setminus R\); it has size ten for a triple and size eight for a
five-set.

The desired coordinated-nine \(r=0\) theorem says that the six prefix
matchings can be replaced, if necessary, by six pairwise edge-disjoint
matchings on the same six prefix supports, after which some three of the
eleven remaining supports admit perfect matchings that are pairwise
edge-disjoint and avoid the repaired prefix.  Thus nine support-respecting
matchings are obtained.

Known rigorous facts:

1. Every remaining size-ten family is live.
2. Any two live size-ten families coordinate unless an edge is forced in
   both; the exact pair gate is already SAT/DRAT-certified.
3. A proposed three-family Helly theorem is false.  There are valid full-row
   instances where three repeated size-ten supports are pairwise compatible
   but have no simultaneous disjoint triple.
4. A stronger global selection theorem using only the seven size-ten rows is
   also false.  There is a valid prefix with
   \(D[U]=K_6-\{e,f\}\), for disjoint \(e,f\), no \(U\)-to-\(W\)
   prefix edges, such that every one of the 35 triples of size-ten
   occurrences fails.  Every mixed choice of two size-ten and one size-eight
   row succeeds.
5. That disconnected extremal is repaired by a support-preserving switch:
   choose the third edge \(g\) completing \(\{e,f,g\}\) to a perfect matching
   of \(U\), and a same-colour edge \(ab\subset W\), then replace
   \(g,ab\) by two cross edges.  All 35 size-ten triples then coordinate.
6. This is not a universal local classification.  Exact search finds bad
   targeted triples in support orbits 2 through 10.  In the exceptional
   orbit with supports \(V\setminus012,V\setminus012,V\setminus034\),
   the residual perfect-matching counts are \(42,42,40\); its disjointness
   tripartite graph is triangle-free but not bipartite; \(D\) has three
   overlapping 12-edge six-cores and no 13-edge six-core.  In its full
   seven-row completion only the targeted triple fails; the other 34 choices
   coordinate.

Do not assume that every bad triple has a \(K_6-2K_2\) core.  Mixed route
types and support-preserving switches are allowed and appear essential.

Please produce one of the following, in decreasing order of value:

1. A complete hand proof of the coordinated-nine \(r=0\) theorem.
2. A finite structural reduction to explicitly stated terminal
   configurations, with proofs of every reduction and repair.
3. A compact exact SAT/DRAT theorem whose UNSAT statement really implies the
   full theorem.  Specify variables and clauses sufficiently precisely to
   audit soundness, including how existential prefix repairs and existential
   three-row continuations are encoded without a quantifier error.

Be especially alert to the quantifier order

\[
\forall\text{ valid input prefix}\quad
\exists\text{ same-support repaired prefix and three-row continuation}.
\]

A naive SAT formula for a bad fixed prefix does not prove that no repair
exists.  A correct certificate may need an exhaustive orbit catalogue with a
separate existential witness per input orbit, or a QBF/game reduction with a
checkable proof format.

State every hypothesis used.  Label computational claims separately.  End
with an explicit verdict: proved, a precisely isolated gap, or a
counterexample.
