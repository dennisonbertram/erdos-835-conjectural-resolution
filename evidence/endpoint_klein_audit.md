# Endpoint-balance Klein-four audit

Status: the most natural canonical Klein-four action is **refuted
exhaustively at \(r=3\)**.

## The \(X\)-configuration graph

Fix a base \(A=S(r-1,r,2r+1)\) and two mates \(B,C\) disjoint from
\(A\).  Let
\[
\Delta=\{P\in A:w_B(P)\ne w_C(P)\}.
\]
An \(X\)-configuration is equivalently an \((r+2)\)-set \(Y\) whose
unique contained \(H_B(P)\) and \(H_C(Q)\) have different base blocks.
Associate to it the edge \(P_LQ_R\) in a bipartite graph
\[
G_{B,C}\quad\text{on}\quad \Delta_L\sqcup\Delta_R.
\]

### Lemma

\(G_{B,C}\) is a simple \((r-1)\)-regular bipartite graph, and its
edges are in canonical bijection with the \(X\)-configurations.

### Proof

Fix \(P\in\Delta\).  The set \(H_B(P)\), of size \(r+1\), has exactly
\(r\) extensions to an \((r+2)\)-set.  One is
\[
P\cup\{w_B(P),w_C(P)\};
\]
it also contains \(H_C(P)\), so its two base blocks agree and it is not
an \(X\)-configuration.  In each of the other \(r-1\) extensions, the
unique contained \(H_C\)-set has a base \(Q\ne P\), giving an
\(X\)-configuration.  Hence every left vertex has degree \(r-1\).
The same argument with \(B,C\) interchanged gives the right degree.

For an edge \(P_LQ_R\), its set \(Y\) must be \(P\cup Q\), so at most
one configuration gives that edge.  Conversely, the defining endpoint
conditions give precisely such an edge. \(\square\)

This also gives the exact count
\[
X(B,C)=(r-1)|\Delta|=(r-1)(b-|B\cap C|).
\]

## The only canonical local involutions at \(r=3\)

At \(r=3\), the graph is 2-regular.  Every edge has a unique other edge
at its left endpoint and a unique other edge at its right endpoint.
Thus there are two canonical fixed-point-free involutions on the
\(X\)-configurations:

- \(\tau_B\): take the other extension of the same \(H_B(P)\);
- \(\tau_C\): take the other extension of the same \(H_C(Q)\).

If these involutions commuted, they would generate a free Klein-four
action and partition \(X\) into four-element orbits.

## Exhaustive result

`verify_H_identity.py` constructs these maps directly for the fixed
Fano plane, its eight mates, and all 56 ordered pairs of distinct
mates.  Exact output:

```text
(4) canonical r=3 fibre involutions:
    0/56 ordered mate pairs commute
    product cycle types: {(6, 6): 56}
    generated-group orbit types: {(12,): 56}
```

For every pair, \(G_{B,C}\) is a single 12-cycle.  The product
\(\tau_B\tau_C\) has two 6-cycles, while the group generated by the two
reflections is transitive on all twelve configurations.  The script
also prints a concrete \(Y\) for which
\(\tau_B\tau_C(Y)\ne\tau_C\tau_B(Y)\).

Thus the observed equality of the four endpoint-bit counts at \(r=3\)
does not arise from these canonical fibre involutions.

## Exact obstruction to generalization

At \(r=15\), every fibre has size \(r-1=14\), so “take the other
extension” is no longer even well-defined.  One would have to choose a
fixed-point-free pairing of the fourteen incident edges at every left
vertex and at every right vertex.

The resulting two edge involutions commute freely exactly when their
orbits form 4-cycles of the bipartite graph.  Hence such a mechanism
requires a compatible decomposition of \(E(G_{B,C})\) into 4-cycles.
But that already implies
\[
4\mid |E(G_{B,C})|=14|\Delta|,
\]
and therefore \(|\Delta|\) is even—the desired mate-intersection
parity itself.  No canonical source for these pairings is supplied by
the \(H\)-system.

Worse, the base case gives a sharp obstruction rather than evidence:
its graph is \(C_{12}\), which contains no 4-cycle at all.  Any viable
endpoint-balance proof must therefore use additional global Steiner
structure, not the canonical “other \(H\)-extension” maps.
